## Permitted without any agreement

✅ Read, copy, modify, and distribute the source code.
✅ Run Didacta in production for **your own internal business purposes** (training your employees, internal LMS, internal documentation portal, etc.).
✅ Run Didacta for **non-commercial or personal use**.
✅ Distribute the software or your modifications **for free, for non-commercial purposes**.
✅ Build proofs of concept, evaluations, audits, demos.
✅ Use Didacta in academic research, theses, courses, learning material.
✅ Contribute back to the project.

## Examples that are fine

- A company installs Didacta to train its 500 employees in compliance, internal tools, onboarding. → ✅ Permitted (internal business use).
- A teacher self-hosts Didacta to share lessons with their own students for free. → ✅ Permitted (non-commercial).
- A consultant deploys Didacta in their own machine to evaluate it for a future client recommendation. → ✅ Permitted (evaluation).
- A university uses Didacta as study material in a course on LMS architecture. → ✅ Permitted (educational, non-commercial).

## Examples that need a commercial agreement

- An academy charges students who learn through Didacta. → ❌ Requires agreement (paid distribution / SaaS-like).
- A consultancy installs Didacta for a paying client and charges hosting fees. → ❌ Requires agreement (paid hosting).
- A SaaS company offers "LMS as a service" using Didacta as the engine. → ❌ Requires agreement.
- A vendor sells Didacta-with-its-logo-removed under a different brand. → ❌ Requires agreement (white-label).

## What about Enterprise features?

Files matching `*.ee.*` and folders `ee/` / `*.ee/` are **NOT** covered by the Community license. They require a Didacta Enterprise License key. Without that key, those features are gated at runtime by the License SDK and cannot be used.

See [`enterprise.md`](enterprise.md) for the full list of Enterprise features.
